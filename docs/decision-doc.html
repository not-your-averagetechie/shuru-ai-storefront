Shuru: AI Storefront Generator

Decision doc · SuperProfile PM (AI-native, 0→1) take-home · Live: shuru-ai-storefront.vercel.app · Code: github.com/not-your-averagetechie/shuru-ai-storefront
In one line: a creator types their Instagram handle (or two lines about themselves), confirms it's them, and about 15 seconds later is editing a finished store: bio, 2–4 products laddered from an impulse buy to a premium offer, INR prices with a private "why this price", and a phone-first page they publish to a link and share on WhatsApp. Buyers tap I want this, which tells the creator which product to make first.

1. The problem, as I understand it

The empty store is not a copywriting problem. A creator with 20K followers can write a caption; what they lack is confidence about three decisions: what is sellable, what to charge, and whether anyone will buy. Every blank field is a decision they postpone, so they stall. The job of this product is to replace decisions with defaults the creator can react to, and to make "publish" feel safe.

Assumptions I made (each is a thing I'd check with SuperProfile data):

  • Most stalled creators have not made a product yet. So the store sells ideas. The public page collects "I want this" (name + WhatsApp/email) instead of payment: it is honest to buyers and it validates demand before the creator builds anything. On SuperProfile this becomes a pre-order through existing checkout.
  • The first sale matters more than margin. Buyers are price-sensitive and pay by UPI, so every store gets an entry product at ₹499 or less.
  • Waiting is where creators drop off. Generation has to finish in ~10–20s with visible progress, on a phone, on patchy data.
  • Persona reframe: I treat both personas as one person at different points, "has knowledge, has never sold", and give them one input screen with two doors (Instagram / Describe myself). The micro-creator gets richer output from their profile; the expert without an audience loses nothing.

4. What I deliberately did not build

  • Payments, delivery, accounts: SuperProfile already has them; rebuilding would spend hours that don't change the activation outcome.
  • AI cover images: slow, costly, and generic stock-looking art lowers trust. Themes + emoji instead.
  • Deep theming / layout editor: four colour themes only. Every extra choice is another place to stall.
  • Testimonials, "500+ students", strike-through prices: they would be invented. The prompt forbids invented facts; the code never adds fake anchors.
  • Reading reels (video/vision) or YouTube: captions + bio carry most of the signal for v1 at a fraction of the cost.
  • Creator dashboard beyond views, taps and leads.

5. How the AI part works

Input. A static system prompt holds the product judgment: the buyer, the ladder rule, allowed formats, INR bands (injected from code so prompt and validator never disagree), writing rules, safety rules and an injection guard. The user message carries the profile (bio, followers, category, last 8 captions) and notes in tagged blocks, the language choice and the audience tier.

Output. JSON schema enforced through Claude structured outputs and parsed with zod: input_check (ok / thin / unusable + one follow-up question), assumptions, creator (name, niche, tagline, bio), theme, language_used, and 2–4 products (format enum, title, price, description, 3 includes, CTA, rationale).

Quality control, in four layers. (1) Before any paid call: handle format, empty input, keyboard-mash heuristics, empty profile. (2) Schema enforced by the API. (3) Business rules: 2–4 products, distinct formats, one entry product ≤ ₹499, price within band → if broken, one repair call listing the violations. (4) Deterministic clean-up: clamp to band, snap to charm price, unit from format, trim lengths, sort into a ladder.

Failure handling. 25s timeout on Haiku → retry on Sonnet (30s) → "AI is slow, try again" with the input kept. Refusals (e.g. guaranteed-returns stock tips) get a kind message. Thin input still produces a store, plus a "we filled in a few gaps" banner and one question that rebuilds the store when answered.

Tuning from real outputs. I ran about a dozen generations across niches and fixed what I saw: every store was e-book + template pack (now max one PDF product); rationales all said "a cup of chai" (one comparison per store); follower counts in descriptions were ignored (tier now inferred from notes); Hinglish copy assumed the creator's gender (neutral phrasing required); a workshop was priced "per session" (units now set by code); rationales cited made-up "market rates" (forbidden).

7. Launch thought

Start where the pain already sits: creators who signed up and never published. SuperProfile knows their Instagram handle, so draft their store before they come back and send it on WhatsApp: "We drafted your store from your Instagram, publish it in one tap." Each draft costs under ₹1 and the lift is measurable within a week. Then make it the default onboarding for new signups behind the A/B above. Later, a public no-login version ("What should you sell? Find out in 60 seconds") becomes top-of-funnel, with "Made with SuperProfile" on every published page as a loop.

8. With two more weeks

  1. Eval set: 40 real creator profiles across niches and languages, a rubric (product fit, price sanity, language, no invented facts) graded by an LLM judge plus spot human review, run on every prompt change.
  2. Real price bands from SuperProfile sales by niche × format × follower tier, replacing my assumptions.
  3. Help the creator make product #1: generate the e-book outline, workshop agenda or WhatsApp-community welcome kit. That is the next stall after publishing.
  4. Wire "I want this" into checkout as UPI pre-orders, with automatic messages to the waitlist when the creator marks the product ready.
  5. Voice-note input in Hindi/Hinglish for creators who talk more easily than they type; Hindi app chrome.

How I used AI coding tools

Claude Code did most of the typing: scaffolding, components, API routes, and driving a browser at phone width to test flows and edge cases. I used it to check the Instagram actor's real output on live handles (including a private and a non-existent one) before writing the parser, and to run batches of generations to compare prompt versions. The product calls (ladder rule, price bands, waitlist instead of checkout, what not to build) and the prompt fixes above came from reading real outputs; I treated the tool's first drafts as drafts. Stack: Next.js 16, Tailwind, Claude API, Apify, Upstash Redis, Vercel.
